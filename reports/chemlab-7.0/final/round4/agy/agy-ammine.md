* **Sources:** R. M. Smith & A. E. Bjerrum, *Metal Ammine Formation in Aqueous Solution* (P. Haase & Son, Copenhagen, 1941).
* **Which set fits an ideal ($I \to 0$) model?** The **Smith & Martell / NIST SRD 46 $I = 0$** column is thermodynamically self-consistent with $I = 0$ $K_w$, $K_a(\mathrm{NH}_4^+)$, and $K_{sp}$. Even though $\mathrm{NH}_3$ is neutral (so Debye–Hückel electrostatic terms cancel between $\mathrm{M}^{2+}$ and $[\mathrm{M(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$), salting-out of neutral $\mathrm{NH}_3$ ($\gamma_{\mathrm{NH}_3} > 1$, raised to the 4th power) and hydration-shell differences shift $\log\beta_4$ upward by ~0.7–0.9 units in $2\text{ M}$ salt media.
* **Include $\beta_5$ for $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$?** **No — omit $\beta_5$.** Because of $d^9$ Jahn–Teller axial elongation, Bjerrum (1941) found $\log K_5 \approx -0.52$ ($K_5 \approx 0.30\text{ M}^{-1}$, at $I = 2\text{ M}$; $I = 0$ value is poorly constrained/uncertain). Since ChemLab's $\mathrm{NH}_3$ stock is $0.5\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{Cu(NH}_3)_5^{2+}]/[\mathrm{Cu(NH}_3)_4^{2+}] = K_5 L < 0.10$ in typical mixtures. $\beta_5$ only matters in concentrated $\mathrm{NH}_3$ ($\gtrsim 2\text{–}5\text{ M}$).

### 2. Numerical Checks (25 °C, Ideal Model: $\gamma_i = 1$)

Auxiliary $I = 0$ constants used: $K_w = 1.0\times 10^{-14}$, $\mathrm{p}K_a(\mathrm{NH}_4^+) = 9.25 \implies K_b(\mathrm{NH}_3) = 1.78\times 10^{-5}$; $K_{sp}(\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2) = 2.2\times 10^{-20}$; $K_{sp}(\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_2) = 3.0\times 10^{-17}$; $K_{sp}(\mathrm{Ag}_2\mathrm{O}) = [\mathrm{Ag}^+][\mathrm{OH}^-] = 2.0\times 10^{-8}$ (Smith & Martell Vol. 4 / NIST SRD 46; some texts use $1.52\times 10^{-8}$); $K_{sp}(\mathrm{AgCl}) = 1.77\times 10^{-10}$; $K_{sp}(\mathrm{AgI}) = 8.5\times 10^{-17}$ (NIST/IUPAC).

#### Key Finding First: Why 0.5 M Unbuffered $\mathrm{NH}_3$ Does **Not** Fully Dissolve $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$ or $\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_2$
At complete dissolution ($s = 0$ precipitate), all dissolved $\mathrm{M}^{2+}$ and $[\mathrm{M(NH}_3)_i]^{2+}$ balance $\mathrm{SO}_4^{2-}$ in charge balance, leaving $[\mathrm{NH}_4^+] = [\mathrm{OH}^-] = \sqrt{K_b L}$ ($\mathrm{pH} \approx 11.34$). The no-precipitate condition $Q = [\mathrm{M}^{2+}][\mathrm{OH}^-]^2 \le K_{sp}$ requires:
$$\frac{C_{\mathrm{M}}}{L^3} \le \frac{K_{sp}\,\beta_4}{K_b}$$
When partial precipitate $s > 0$ remains, precipitation generates $[\mathrm{NH}_4^+] = 2s + [\mathrm{OH}^-] \approx 2s$, buffering pH down to ~10.0 and letting a substantial fraction dissolve — **but trying to dissolve the last of the solid consumes the $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ buffer, spiking $[\mathrm{OH}^-]$ ~20× and locking the remaining solid in place.**

### 3. Is $K_{sp} = 2.2 \times 10^{-20}$ the Right Value for $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$?

* **Provenance:** $2.2 \times 10^{-20}$ ($\mathrm{p}K_{sp} = 19.66$) is a standard general-chemistry textbook figure (Zumdahl, Tro, Brown–LeMay, traced to older aged/crystalline compilations such as Latimer).
* **Critical compilations ($25\,^\circ\text{C}$, $I = 0$):**
  * **NIST SRD 46 / Smith & Martell Vol. 4 (1976):** $\mathrm{p}K_{sp} = 19.32$ ($K_{sp} = \mathbf{4.8 \times 10^{-20}}$) for crystalline $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$.
  * **Feitknecht & Schindler (1963, *Pure Appl. Chem.* 6, 130–199, IUPAC)** and **Lange's Handbook** (certain editions): distinguish **freshly precipitated ("active"/amorphous) $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$** at $\mathrm{p}K_{sp} \approx 18.8$ ($K_{sp} \approx \mathbf{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$) from aged crystalline $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$ ($\mathrm{p}K_{sp} \approx 19.3\text{–}19.7$) and dehydrated $\mathrm{CuO}$ (tenorite, $\mathrm{p}K_{sp} \approx 20.3\text{–}20.4$).
* **Verdict:** For a real-time benchtop titration where precipitates are seconds old, pairing NIST $I=0$ $\beta_n$ with **fresh-precipitate $K_{sp} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$** (or NIST $4.8 \times 10^{-20}$) is physically better than $2.2 \times 10^{-20}$ (and similarly fresh amorphous $\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_2$ has $K_{sp} \approx 3\times 10^{-16}$ vs crystalline $\varepsilon$-$\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_2$ $3\times 10^{-17}$). **However, as Section 2 proves, no physically defensible $K_{sp}$ makes 50 mL of 0.5 M unbuffered $\mathrm{NH}_3$ completely dissolve 5 mL of 0.5 M $\mathrm{CuSO}_4$ or $\mathrm{ZnSO}_4$.** To reproduce the textbook "complete dissolution" demonstration honestly without fudging constants, ChemLab needs either a standard benchtop **2–6 M $\mathrm{NH}_3$** reagent, more dilute $\mathrm{CuSO}_4$/$\mathrm{ZnSO}_4$ (~0.05 M), or an $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ salt present.

### 5. Physical Pitfalls & Limitations to State in a School-Level Simulation

1. **Basic copper(II) sulfate precipitation instead of pure $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$:** Adding $\mathrm{NH}_3$ to $\mathrm{CuSO}_4$ at low $\mathrm{NH}_3:\mathrm{Cu}$ actually precipitates pale greenish-blue basic sulfates (brochantite $\mathrm{Cu}_4(\mathrm{OH})_6\mathrm{SO}_4$, antlerite $\mathrm{Cu}_3(\mathrm{OH})_4\mathrm{SO}_4$) before pure $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$, altering both stoichiometry ($1.5\text{ vs } 2\;\mathrm{OH}^-/\mathrm{Cu}$) and dissolution threshold.
2. **Ideal-solution ($\gamma_i = 1$) error & $\mathrm{MSO}_4^0$ ion pairing:** In $0.05\text{–}0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{CuSO}_4$/$\mathrm{ZnSO}_4$ ($I \approx 0.2\text{–}2.0\text{ M}$), $\gamma_{\mathrm{M}^{2+}}\gamma_{\mathrm{OH}^-}^2 \approx 0.05\text{–}0.25$, making effective concentration-based $K_{sp}'$ **4–20× higher** than thermodynamic $K_{sp}$, while strong ion pairing ($\log K_{\mathrm{assoc}}(\mathrm{MSO}_4^0) \approx 2.3$ at $I=0$) further lowers free $[\mathrm{M}^{2+}]$. Ideal concentrations therefore systematically **overpredict hydroxide precipitation**.
3. **Unbuffered $\mathrm{NH}_3$ dissolution paradox:** As shown in Section 2, dilute unbuffered $\mathrm{NH}_3$ (0.5 M) cannot finish dissolving $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$ or $\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_2$ at $0.05\text{ M}$ total metal because eliminating the solid destroys the $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ buffer and raises pH to ~11.35. Real qualitative analysis uses 2–15 M $\mathrm{NH}_3$ or adds $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ salts.
4. **Precipitate aging & dehydration:** Fresh amorphous $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$/$\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_2$ are ~5–10× more soluble than aged crystalline forms, and $\mathrm{Cu(OH)}_2$ slowly dehydrates (faster on heating) to black $\mathrm{CuO}$, which is far less soluble in $\mathrm{NH}_3$.
5. **Pentaammine $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ omission:** Negligible at $\le 0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{NH}_3$, but becomes relevant if concentrated $\mathrm{NH}_3$ ($\ge 2\text{–}5\text{ M}$) is added later.
6. **Mixed hydroxo–ammine & polynuclear species (plus charge-balance check):** Splitting $\alpha_{\text{hyd}} + \sum \beta_i L^i$ omits mixed species (e.g., $[\mathrm{Cu(NH}_3)_n(\mathrm{OH})]^+$) and polynuclear hydrolysis (e.g., $\mathrm{Cu}_2(\mathrm{OH})_2^{2+}$). Also ensure the engine gives hydrolyzed species in $\alpha_{\text{hyd}}$ their **actual reduced/anionic charges** (e.g., $\mathrm{Zn(OH)}_4^{2-}$) in the charge balance, unlike $[\mathrm{M(NH}_3)_i]^{z+}$ which keeps charge $z+$.
7. **$\mathrm{NH}_3$ volatility & $\mathrm{CO}_2$ uptake:** Open-beaker $\mathrm{NH}_3$ outgasses and absorbs atmospheric $\mathrm{CO}_2$, slowly lowering $[\mathrm{NH}_3]$, buffering pH, and potentially forming basic copper carbonate.
8. **Lab safety note (ammoniacal $\mathrm{Ag}^+$):** Tollens-like $[\mathrm{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+$ solutions can form shock-sensitive explosive silver nitride ($\mathrm{Ag}_3\mathrm{N}$) upon standing or drying — worth flagging in student-facing notes.
